Dear Editors,

We submit "What Administrative Penalty Documents Measure: A Validation Audit of Food-Safety Enforcement Data in China" as an Original Research article.

Chinese administrative penalty decisions are the most abundant machine-readable record of food-safety enforcement, and studies count them without establishing what they measure. We audited the record as an instrument on a frozen corpus of 1,128 documents: 875 Chinese penalty decisions and 253 US recall records.

Three results bear on how surveillance tables are built. To our knowledge this is the first audit of penalty-decision text as a measurement instrument carried out under a pre-registered design, with a negative control attached to every derived figure. The food dimension then splits in two: 0.9296 of lexicon matches mention the named commodity, but only 0.3852 are about it, so a table of mentions overstates every category it lists. Finally, the estimability ladder is a transferable protocol: it fixes which questions a record can answer, with the measurement that licenses each verdict.

We believe this fits the journal's interest in the measurement basis of public-health surveillance.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere.

Songze Zhu, on behalf of all authors
